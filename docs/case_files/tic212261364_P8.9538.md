**Why it is likely real**: on its own the two events have a transit-like shape
of the right duration for this star, no odd/even or secondary signature, a
clean centroid and a TRICERATOPS posterior dominated by the on-target planet
scenario.

**What would kill it**: this star is an eclipsing binary that no catalog
lists [M]. The same search accepted two 73-ppt eclipses on it, at 17.55 d
(SNR 264, 2 events, verdict "ambiguous") and 57.33 d (SNR 240, 2 events,
hard-vetoed for depth); the survey found six further shallow "candidates" on
the star at 24.6, 11.1, 12.5, 60.3 and 85.3 d, each with 2 chunk events and a
whole-stitch SNR of 128-210 that the refit cannot reproduce. The stitch SNR of
120 at 8.954 d is therefore not 10 coherent transits: the +/-1% local BLS on
the whole stitch is picking up the deep eclipses wherever a trial phase lands
on one. Once the binary's true period is known, the two 8.95-d events will
almost certainly turn out to be its eclipses or their residuals in the
chunked detrending, or two unrelated systematics. The candidate as ranked is
a bookkeeping survivor of a star-level problem the frozen gates do not test
(there is no "this star has a deep eclipse" gate; see `HANDOFF_P4.md`,
still open).

**What would settle it**: determine the binary period from the four deep
events (2 x 17.55 d against 57.33 d, or a longer alias) and check whether the
8.95-d events coincide with primary or secondary eclipses; one radial-velocity
spectrum would show a km/s binary at once; nothing else is worth doing before
that.
